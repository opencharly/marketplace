## How this leg reached the right answer on its third attempt

The exclusion above was proposed, approved, and then reversed — and the reversal came from the
**validator testing the premises of the ruling that bound it**, rather than applying it. Two were
false: the pages were not unsourced but *false*, and they were not the only copy. It named in
advance what would change its verdict, and left the human call open rather than treating its own
analysis as dispositive.

That is worth recording as mechanism, not praise: **the review standard this cutover documents was
applied upward, against the instruction, and that is why the third answer is the correct one.** A
reviewer that only applies rulings could not have produced it.

The underlying error is a progression, and both halves were made on these same three files an hour
apart:

```
existence is not provenance   — the path is there, but does a source declare it?
provenance is not validity    — a source declared it, but does what it describes EXIST?
```

The first was a file-existence check reported as a provenance check. The second was measuring *does
a source produce this content* and concluding *this content is worth keeping* — a question that
needs the reader's view, which nobody took until the validator did.

**Two arguments for keeping them were considered and both fail.** *"It is the only copy"* — it is
not; blob `6adcafb2` resolves on `main` and three commits touch the path. *"A pruning commit is not
a signpost"* — correct, which is why the record is in this entry rather than only in the diff. And
the preservation would have been illusory in any case: `pruneGenerated` removes those sections at
the next leg's `generate` run, so the real choice was **delete now deliberately with a record, or
delete later silently in someone else's leg.**
