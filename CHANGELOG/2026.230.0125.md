# 2026.230.0125 — the mechanism grouping drops the universal that its new member falsified

Projection of `opencharly/charly#289`'s source fix. One file, `+4/-3`.

## Both universals are removed, not one

The grouping carried a second — *"They are not instances of the question"* — and it is falsifiable
**immediately**, by reading alone, rather than on the next append: sections 12 and 13 *can* be
re-described that way. A cold reader did exactly that.

**Deleted rather than defended.** The grouping is complete without it: the positive definition
plus the checked-by-running test already carry the split, so the negative clause adds no
information and supplies the only attack surface. Two universals on one sentence, both falsified
within hours, is the pattern naming its own remedy.

**The section stays in mechanism.** The reading that would move it proves too much — *"I measured X
and it is not the thing my claim is about"* re-describes **every** mechanism section, since each
exists to correct an inference drawn from a measurement. A distinction that dissolves under its own
argument is not the one the page draws. The page's own test decides: *an epistemic claim is checked
by reasoning; a mechanism claim only by running the thing* — and you cannot reason your way to
"the guard must run in both repos."

## The universal was the defect, not the membership

`charly#289` added the vacuous-guard section to this page's mechanism grouping, leaving the
closing clause reading *"They are not instances of the question **and no frame unifies them**."*
That went false on arrival: the guard section and the validator-status section state one frame
between them — **an examination that did not happen is not a result**.

A list carrying *no frame unifies them* **re-asserts that of every member ever appended to it**,
silently, by anyone, forever. So the clause is removed rather than re-qualified. What remains —
*they are not instances of the question* — is the claim the epistemic/mechanism split exists to
make, and it survives future members.

## Two adjacent fixes in the same page

A `###` heading sat flush against the paragraph above it, so it may not render as a heading at
all — the structural fingerprint of an append that swept nothing. **Only one heading needed this**:
a first check flagged three, but two of those open a `content: |` block and have nothing
preceding them in the rendered page. The check was corrected before it was believed.

And *"The same key produced both a miss and a false positive"* then quoted **two different keys**,
`cmd:"[a-z_-]` and `cmd:"[a-z_]`. Which grep truly ran is not recoverable from the record, so
the identity claim is dropped rather than guessed — the lesson never depended on the two keys
being byte-identical.
