## Proximity to a rule provides no protection

This entry's own first draft was titled *"the **last** CI-gate claim"* — a completeness claim about
its own sweep, one line above the correction of a page for counting its own sections. The sweep
appeared to support it, and a later differently-keyed sweep refuted it outright: `CLAUDE.md` carried
*"CI-enforced by `charly/import_purity_test.go`"* — the same class a third time, invisible to any key
derived from the phrasing of a site already found, and absent from `AGENTS.md`, so the two rulebooks
diverged on exactly that sentence. **A sweep enumerates the phrasings its author thought of, and the
class — *asserts a CI gate that does not exist* — has no closed set of them. "Nothing found" is not
"none exists" — and here it was not even "nothing found."**

That is worth stating as a property rather than an apology: **a document is not protected from a
defect by describing it.** The same page carries the rule, the counterexample, and the correction,
and the rule did not prevent the counterexample from being written directly above it. Whatever makes
these defects hard to see is not addressed by knowing about them — which is the argument for
mechanical checks over attentional ones, made by the failure of an attentional one.

**Every individual round was correct.** Each finding was real, each fix addressed what it named, and

invisible from inside any step** — which is why nothing in the round-by-round process caught it, and
why the measurement that did was a trend across eight commits rather than an inspection of one.

The mechanism, stated so it transfers:

> **A fix that adds explanation creates a new surface with the same failure mode as the defect it

**The detectable signature is monotonic growth**, and it does not care which document the growth is
in: *when a change's diff grows every round while its defect count does not fall, the fixes are the
