# 2026.296.1153 — fix(body-lint): the headline paragraph must not lose its first character

## Summary

Head `949325db048651f6bc0cb6fbbcf214317697747d`, **2 files, +20 −1** — the body linter's
`diffstat` family no longer loses the first character of a headline that IS the body's first
paragraph, so a wrong headline at the very top of a body is reported instead of silently
skipped.

**The finding.** The `diffstat` family finds the headline paragraph with
`text.rfind("\n\n", 0, head.start()) + 2` — the paragraph that starts after the last blank line
before the head. When the headline IS the body's first paragraph there is no preceding blank
line, `rfind` returns `-1`, and the old `+ 2` turned that into `1`: the paragraph began at
index 1 and its first character was dropped. A diffstat that OPENS the paragraph — `2 files,
+33 −8` — lost its leading digit, matched nothing, and the rule skipped it.

**The mechanism.** The failure is silent and it is the one failure mode a rule like this may
not have: the rule reports a wrong headline in the middle of a body and says nothing about a
wrong headline at the top of one. The family was not wrong about the diff — it was asking the
wrong question of the text.

**The fix.** The paragraph now starts at `0` when there is no preceding blank line
(`sep = text.rfind("\n\n", 0, head.start()); start = 0 if sep == -1 else sep + 2`), and a
regression test covers the case. The test is RED on the old form and GREEN on the new one.

**Why this is a carry-forward.** The defect was found in the review of
`opencharly/marketplace#548`, whose head was already merged when it was found, so it could not
ride in that PR. This PR is its successor: it names #548 as its predecessor, and the issue
`opencharly/marketplace#547` — which #548 closed — records the same carry-forward.
