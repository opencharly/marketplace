### `344 of 350` has two preimages, and that is the finding

Two of this entry's figures were flagged as **stale** — true three commits earlier and relabelled
`plugins@01edd45e` without re-derivation. Re-deriving, they are also true **at** `01edd45e` under
`-- '*.md'`. Both readings are correct, because the integer has two preimages:

```
matcher: "DO NOT EDIT" spaced, case-sensitive, counting hits

ref          unrestricted     -- '*.md'
204ff55c     344 of 350       343 of 349
da76ec0f     344 of 350       343 of 349
01edd45e     345 of 351       344 of 350
```

The file triple behaves identically: `346 + 6 - 4 = 348` is md-only at `01edd45e`, and
`347 + 6 - 4 = 349` is unrestricted there.

**So the defect is neither staleness nor an undeclared pathspec. It is that the figure does not
identify its own `(ref, scope)` pair, and therefore cannot be re-derived from what the sentence
states.** A reader handed `344 of 350` and the label `01edd45e` cannot tell which of two true
measurements they hold, and both diagnoses of it were right about a different one.

**The correction attempt reproduced the defect facing the other way.** The first fix here replaced
`344 of 350` with `345 of 351` — one true figure for another true figure, silently changing the
population, which is precisely what the original sentence did. **Declaring the axis matters more
than getting the integer right**, because a declared figure that is wrong gets caught and an
undeclared figure that is right does not.

The tell was in this entry the whole time: its was/is table already carried `344/350` **and**
`345/351` in one cell, at "98.29% both" — two populations presented as one correction. Proofreading
the table passes and proofreading the prose passes, because a table and its prose assert more
together than either does alone.

**One file carries both ambiguities.** `setup` is the only non-`.md` carrier at `01edd45e`, so it
is the entire difference between the unrestricted and `*.md` columns — and it is also the `#`-form
carrier that appears when the matcher is restricted to `<!--` markers, where the count becomes

**Six axes are now named rather than resolved**, since resolving them belongs to the page rather
